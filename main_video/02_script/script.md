# The Army That Lost a War to Birds

## Narration

Australian soldiers once marched into battle against a flock of birds.

In 1932, drought drove thousands of emus into Western Australia’s wheat country. They tore through crops, broke fences, and vanished across the open dust before anyone could stop them.

The farmers asked the government for help. Australia answered with Major Meredith, artillery soldiers, and Lewis machine guns.

The plan was simple: find the flock, aim the guns, and end the bird problem.

Then the emus arrived.

They split, sprinted, doubled back, and scattered in every direction. The soldiers were aiming at a moving storm of feathers—not a parade ground.

Near a watering point, more than a thousand emus came into view. The ambush was ready.

Then the machine gun jammed.

The flock disappeared into the dust, leaving the soldiers chasing birds across the country.

The operation was withdrawn. The emus had not captured a city. They had simply made machine guns useless.

In the end, farmers turned to fences.

Australia’s strangest war was lost to birds—and solved with better barriers.
